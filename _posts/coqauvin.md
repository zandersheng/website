# Coq au Vin

A rustic French chicken braise made for feeding a crowd. Boneless chicken thighs soak overnight in red wine, then are browned in bacon fat and slowly braised with aromatic vegetables. The sauce is finished with beurre manié for a glossy, velvety consistency.

## Ingredients

### Chicken and Marinade
- 6–8 lb boneless, skinless chicken thighs
- 1 bottle red wine (750 ml), or enough to surround the chicken in the marinating bag
- Fresh thyme or rosemary, optional

### Braising Base
- ½ lb bacon, diced (use more for a richer, smokier flavor)
- 3 yellow onions, diced
- 3 carrots, diced, or more to taste
- 6–8 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- Chicken stock, approximately half the volume of wine used, as needed
- 2–3 bay leaves, optional
- Dried thyme or other herbs, to taste
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper
- All-purpose flour, optional, for dredging the chicken

### Beurre Manié
- 2 tbsp softened unsalted butter
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour

### To Finish
- Reserved crispy bacon
- Fresh parsley or other fresh herbs, chopped

## Instructions

### 1. Marinate the chicken

Place the chicken thighs in a large food-safe zip-top bag. Pour in the red wine, adding enough to surround the chicken. Add fresh thyme or rosemary if desired.

Seal the bag, place it in a dish to catch any leaks, and refrigerate overnight.

### 2. Render the bacon

Remove the chicken from the marinade, reserving the wine. Pat the chicken thoroughly dry with paper towels.

In a large Dutch oven or heavy-bottomed pot, cook the diced bacon over medium heat until crisp and the fat has rendered. Remove the bacon with a slotted spoon and reserve it for serving. Leave the rendered fat in the pot.

### 3. Brown the chicken

Season the chicken with salt and pepper. If desired, lightly dredge the thighs in flour, shaking off the excess.

Working in batches, brown the chicken in the bacon fat until golden on the outside. Avoid overcrowding the pot; depending on its size and your stove, this may take four or more batches.

Transfer the browned chicken to a tray or plate. It does not need to be cooked through yet.

### 4. Build the aromatic base

Add the onions and carrots to the same pot. Cook until they begin to soften, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom.

Add the garlic and cook until fragrant. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for another minute or two.

Deglaze with chicken stock, scraping the bottom of the pot to release the fond.

### 5. Reduce the wine

Meanwhile, pour the reserved marinade into a separate saucepan. Bring it to a full boil, then simmer until reduced by approximately half and slightly syrupy.

Because the wine was in contact with raw chicken, it must be thoroughly boiled before being incorporated into the braise.

### 6. Braise the chicken

Pour the reduced wine into the Dutch oven. Return the browned chicken and any accumulated juices to the pot.

Add bay leaves, dried thyme, or other herbs as desired. Add chicken stock, starting at roughly half the volume of wine used, and adjust as needed so the chicken is surrounded by enough braising liquid.

Bring to a gentle simmer. Cover with a lid or use a cartouche to regulate evaporation.

Braise for approximately 30–40 minutes, or until the chicken is tender and fully cooked. Check that the thickest pieces reach at least 165°F (74°C).

### 7. Finish the sauce with beurre manié

Remove the chicken temporarily and keep it warm. Discard the bay leaves.

In a small bowl, mash the softened butter and flour together with a fork until a smooth paste forms.

Bring the braising liquid to a gentle simmer. Whisk in the beurre manié a little at a time, allowing each addition to dissolve before adding more. Simmer for several minutes, stirring, until the sauce thickens and loses its raw-flour taste.

Use only as much beurre manié as needed to achieve a rich, velvety sauce. Return the chicken to the pot and gently warm through.

Taste and adjust the seasoning.

### 8. Garnish and serve

Serve directly from the Dutch oven or transfer to a large serving dish. Scatter the reserved crispy bacon over the chicken and finish with chopped fresh parsley or other fresh herbs.

Serve with mashed potatoes, buttered noodles, or crusty bread to soak up the sauce.

## Notes

- **Wine:** Côtes du Rhône is a dependable choice. Pinot Noir or Beaujolais also work well. Choose a dry wine you would enjoy drinking.
- **Chicken cuts:** Boneless thighs make serving a crowd convenient. Bone-in thighs or drumsticks work too, but require additional cooking time.
- **Gluten-free:** Skip the flour dredge and replace the flour-based beurre manié with a suitable gluten-free thickener.
- **Make ahead:** The braise can be made ahead and gently reheated. The sauce may thicken in the refrigerator; loosen it with a splash of stock when reheating.
- **Leftovers:** Refrigerate promptly and consume within 3–4 days.
